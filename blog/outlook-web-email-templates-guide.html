Email templates in Outlook Web: every option compared (2026)

June 10, 2026

If you answer the same questions by email every day, templates pay for themselves within a week. Here is every realistic way to get them in Outlook on the web (outlook.office.com and outlook.live.com), with the trade-offs spelled out.

1. My Templates (built in)

Outlook Web ships with a template add-in: while composing, open the Apps icon and pick My Templates. It works and costs nothing.

The catch: all templates share a 32 KB total cap, there are no folders, no placeholders, no import or export, and inserting takes several clicks every time. Fine for three short replies; painful beyond that.

2. Signatures as fake templates

Some people save whole reply texts as extra signatures and swap them in. It works in a pinch, but signatures were not built for this: you can only insert one at a time, switching is clumsy, and your actual signature gets replaced.

3. Quick Parts and .oft files (classic desktop only)

Classic desktop Outlook has Quick Parts and .oft template files - genuinely powerful. But neither exists in Outlook on the web or in new Outlook. If your company has moved you to the browser, this door is closed.

4. General-purpose text expanders

Tools like Text Blaze or TextExpander expand abbreviations on any website, Outlook included. They are mature and feature-rich.

The catch: they are subscription products that sync your snippets to their cloud and require an account. Outlook is also not their home turf - formatting quirks in Outlook Web come up in their own support forums. If you only need templates in Outlook, you are paying monthly for a lot of machinery you will not use.

5. An Outlook-focused snippet extension

The remaining option is a small browser extension that does one thing: store snippets and insert them into Outlook Web.

SnipMail takes this approach. Type / plus a shortcut in any email - a picker appears, Enter inserts the text at the cursor. Snippets are stored locally in the browser (nothing is uploaded, no account), there is no 32 KB ceiling, and the free version covers 5 snippets. Pro is planned as a one-time purchase, not a subscription.
