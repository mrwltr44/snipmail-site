Canned responses in Outlook: reply to routine emails in seconds

June 10, 2026

Support agents, recruiters, salespeople and team leads all know the feeling: the tenth "thanks, received, will get back to you by Friday" of the day, typed by hand again. Gmail folks have had canned responses for years. Here is how to build the same workflow in Outlook Web - in about ten minutes.

Step 1: find your real top five

Do not guess. Open your Sent folder and scroll a week back. The replies you actually keep retyping show up immediately. Typical winners:

  • acknowledgement - "got it, I'll reply by [day]"
  • meeting proposal with your booking link
  • "please send the missing details" checklist
  • polite decline
  • follow-up nudge after no reply

Step 2: write them once, properly

A canned response reads as canned only when it is sloppy. Spend two minutes per template: trim the throat-clearing, keep one clear ask per email, and leave a visible gap like [NAME] where personalization belongs - so you never send "Hi ," to a client.

Step 3: give them shortcuts you'll remember

Short, predictable, lowercase: /ack, /meet, /info, /no, /fup. The naming rule that works: the first word you would say out loud when describing the reply.

Step 4: insert them without leaving the keyboard

Outlook's built-in My Templates panel can store short replies, but inserting one means opening the Apps menu, finding the panel and clicking through - and the whole add-in is capped at 32 KB for all templates combined.

With SnipMail (a free Chrome extension for Outlook Web) the flow is: hit Reply, type /ack, press Enter. The response lands at your cursor and you only edit the personal bits. Snippets are stored locally in your browser - no account, no cloud, nothing leaves your machine.

Step 5: review monthly

Once a month, glance at your snippets. Retire the ones you stopped using, fix the wording you keep editing after insertion - if you correct the same phrase every time, the template should change, not you.

The payoff

Five canned responses used eight times a day, two minutes saved each: that is roughly an hour and twenty minutes a week back, for ten minutes of setup. Few productivity tricks have that ratio.
